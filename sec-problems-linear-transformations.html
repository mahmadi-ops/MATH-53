    Exercise Group.

    Problems for Transformations and Linear Transformations (Section 3.1).
    🔗

    1. A Transformation from Two of Its Values.

    Let \(T : \R^3 \to \R^4\) be a linear transformation with
    \begin{equation*} T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 2 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Find \(T \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    2. When Two Values Are Not Enough.

    Let \(T : \R^3 \to \R^3\) be a linear transformation with
    \begin{equation*} T \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Find \(T \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(T \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    3. The Matrix from Two Values.

    A linear transformation \(T : \R^2 \to \R^2\) satisfies
    \begin{equation*} T \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix}, \qquad T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} c \\ d \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Find the matrix of \(T\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Composing Two Transformations.

    Let \(S, T : \R^2 \to \R^2\) be linear transformations with
    \begin{equation*} S \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}, \quad S \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}, \quad T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Find \((S \circ T) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    5. ★ A Transformation from \(\R^3\) to \(\R^4\).

    Let \(T : \R^3 \to \R^4\) be defined by
    \begin{equation*} T \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x - z \\ x + y \\ 2y \\ y + z \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Identify the domain and codomain, and compute the image of \(\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    6. ★ The Previous Transformation Is a Matrix Transformation.

    Show that the transformation of Exercise 10.3.5 is a matrix transformation, and find its matrix.
    🔗
    🔗

    7. ★ Transformation by Matrix Multiplication.

    Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp -3 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Describe the effect of \(A\) on a general vector, and compute the image of \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -4 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    8. ★ Determining a Transformation from Two Values.

    Let \(T : \R^3 \to \R^4\) be linear with
    \begin{equation*} T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 0 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad T \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ -1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
    Find \(T \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    9. ★ Transformations That Are Not Matrix Transformations.

    Give two transformations \(\R^2 \to \R^2\) that are not matrix transformations, and explain why each fails.
