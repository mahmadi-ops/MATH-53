    Chapter 7 Vector Spaces

    Every argument we have made about \(\R^n\) used only a handful of properties of addition and scalar multiplication. Promoting that handful to a definition lets the same theory apply to polynomials, matrices, and much else. The payoff is coordinates: once a basis is chosen, an abstract vector space behaves exactly like \(\R^n\text{,}\) and an abstract linear transformation becomes a matrix.
    🔗
    The chapter closes with two applications in which the vectors are photographs. Two pictures of the same cat, alive in one and dead in the other, are combined into the superpositions that quantum mechanics describes, using nothing beyond the span, basis, and coordinates developed here. Then the question is turned around: instead of making new pictures out of old ones, a machine is built that looks at a picture and decides what it shows—a first step into image recognition, powered by the same coordinates together with the dot product of the previous chapter.
