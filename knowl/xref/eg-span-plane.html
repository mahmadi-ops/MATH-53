Example 2.26. Describing a Span.

Describe the span of the vectors \(\vec{u} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Note that any linear combination of \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) is of the form
\begin{equation*} a_1 \vec{u} + a_2 \vec{v} = a_1 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + a_2 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ a_1 + 2a_2 \\ 2a_1 + 3a_2 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
which can be thought of as a vector in the \(yz\)-plane.
🔗
Also, consider an arbitrary vector \(\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}\) in the \(yz\)-plane. We can write any such vector as a linear combination of \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\text{.}\) Solving
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = x_1 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + x_2 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
amounts to row reducing
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 1 \amp 2 \amp y \\ 2 \amp 3 \amp z \end{array} \right] \longrightarrow \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 1 \amp 2 \amp y \\ 0 \amp -1 \amp z - 2y \end{array} \right] \longrightarrow \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 1 \amp 0 \amp -3y + 2z \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 2y - z \end{array} \right], \end{equation*}
giving \(x_1 = -3y + 2z\) and \(x_2 = 2y - z\text{.}\) That is,
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = (-3y + 2z) \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + (2y - z) \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Hence \(\operatorname{Span}\{ \vec{u}, \vec{v} \}\) is the \(yz\)-plane.
