Example 3.3. A Transformation from \(\R^3\) to \(\R^4\).

Consider \(T : \R^3 \to \R^4\) defined by
\begin{equation*} T \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x + y \\ y \\ y - z \\ -y + z \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
This transformation transforms \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \in \R^3\) to \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \in \R^4\text{.}\)
🔗
In the language of the definition, \(T : \R^3 \to \R^4\) with \(T(\vec{x}) = \vec{b}\text{,}\) where \(\vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} \in \R^3\) and \(\vec{b} = \begin{bmatrix} x+y \\ y \\ y-z \\ -y+z \end{bmatrix} \in \R^4\text{.}\)
