Exercise 12. ★ Finding Eigenvalues.

Find the eigenvalues of \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 3 \amp 0 \\ 2 \amp -4 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Form \(\lambda I - A\) and expand along its third column, which has a single nonzero entry:
\begin{align*} c_A(\lambda) \amp = \det \begin{bmatrix} \lambda - 1 \amp -2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp \lambda - 3 \amp 0 \\ -2 \amp 4 \amp \lambda - 2 \end{bmatrix}\\ \amp = (\lambda - 2) \begin{vmatrix} \lambda - 1 \amp -2 \\ 0 \amp \lambda - 3 \end{vmatrix}\\ \amp = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 3). \end{align*}
🔗
The eigenvalues are therefore \(\lambda = 1\text{,}\) \(2\text{,}\) \(3\text{,}\) each of multiplicity one. As a check, their sum \(1 + 2 + 3 = 6\) equals the trace \(1 + 3 + 2 = 6\) of \(A\text{.}\)
