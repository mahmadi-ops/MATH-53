Example 7.19. The Same Vector in Two Coordinate Systems.

Let \(\mathcal{B}_1 = \{ \vec{e}_1, \vec{e}_2 \}\) be the standard basis of \(\R^2\) and let \(\mathcal{B}_2 = \{ \vec{b}_1, \vec{b}_2 \}\) be a second basis, related to the first by
\begin{equation*} \vec{e}_1 = \tfrac{1}{2}\vec{b}_1, \qquad \vec{e}_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\vec{b}_1 + \vec{b}_2. \end{equation*}
🔗
A vector \(\vec{x}\) with \([\vec{x}]_{\mathcal{B}_1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) i.e., \(\vec{x} = \vec{e}_1 + 2\vec{e}_2\text{,}\) has
\begin{equation*} [\vec{x}]_{\mathcal{B}_2} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{3}{2} \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \text{i.e.} \qquad \vec{x} = \tfrac{3}{2}\vec{b}_1 + 2\vec{b}_2. \end{equation*}
The vector \(\vec{x}\) has not moved; only the grid we measure it against has changed.
🔗
Solving the two relations above for \(\vec{b}_1\) and \(\vec{b}_2\) gives
\begin{equation*} \vec{b}_1 = 2\vec{e}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \vec{b}_2 = \vec{e}_2 - \vec{e}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
so \(\mathcal{B}_2\) rules the plane with a grid of parallelograms rather than squares.
