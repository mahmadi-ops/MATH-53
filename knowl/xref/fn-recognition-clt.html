Footnote 7.9.

The exactness of this formula leans on the assumption that the grain is Gaussian: a sum of independent normal terms is exactly normal. Real sensor grain is not Gaussian. But the score \(G \cdot W\) is a sum of \(48\) independent contributions, and the central limit theorem—the statement that such sums come out approximately normal whatever each contribution looks like—is what makes the formula approximately right anyway. That robustness, and not the Gaussian special case, is why the normal model here is a mild assumption rather than a fragile one.
