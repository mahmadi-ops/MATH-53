Example 9.1. Alice’s Lunch.

Alice always eats lunch either at restaurant \(A\) or restaurant \(B\text{.}\) Suppose that
  • Initially she is equally likely to eat at each restaurant.
    🔗
    🔗
  • She never eats at \(A\) two days in a row.
    🔗
    🔗
  • If she eats at restaurant \(B\) one day, then the next day she is three times as likely to eat at \(B\) as at \(A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
Calculate the probabilities of her eating at \(A\) and \(B\) for the first week. What do you notice?
🔗
Solution.
The initial state vector is \(\vec{S}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2} \\ \frac{1}{2} \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and the transition matrix is
\begin{equation*} T = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp \frac{1}{4} \\ 1 \amp \frac{3}{4} \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
where the columns are indexed by today’s restaurant (\(A\text{,}\) then \(B\)) and the rows by the next day’s.
🔗
Iterating \(\vec{S}_{k+1} = T\vec{S}_k\text{,}\)
\begin{align*} \vec{S}_1 \amp = T\vec{S}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.125 \\ 0.875 \end{bmatrix},\\ \vec{S}_2 \amp = T\vec{S}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2188 \\ 0.7812 \end{bmatrix},\\ \amp \;\; \vdots\\ \vec{S}_6 \amp = T\vec{S}_5 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2001 \\ 0.7999 \end{bmatrix},\\ \vec{S}_7 \amp = T\vec{S}_6 = \begin{bmatrix} 0.2001 \\ 0.7999 \end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
🔗
The state vectors seem to be converging to \(\begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Also, we tried changing the initial state vector and it seemed that the transition matrix above causes the state vectors to converge to \(\begin{bmatrix} 0.2 \\ 0.8 \end{bmatrix}\) independent of the choice of \(\vec{S}_0\text{.}\)
