Example 9.11. Eigenvalues of a Stochastic Matrix Satisfy \(|\lambda| \leq 1\).

Show that eigenvalues of a stochastic matrix \(A\) satisfy \(|\lambda| \leq 1\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Assume \(\vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{bmatrix}\) is an eigenvector of \(A^T\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda\text{,}\) and assume that \(x_i\) is the entry of \(\vec{x}\) with largest absolute value, so \(|x_j| \leq |x_i|\) for all \(j\text{.}\)
🔗
Writing \(A = \begin{bmatrix} a_{ij} \end{bmatrix}\) so that \(A^T = \begin{bmatrix} a_{ji} \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and focusing on the \(i\)th entry of \(\lambda \vec{x} = A^T\vec{x}\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \left| \lambda x_i \right| = \left| \sum_j a_{ji} x_j \right| \leq \sum_j \left| a_{ji} x_j \right| = \sum_j a_{ji} |x_j| \leq \underbrace{\sum_j a_{ji}}_{= \, 1} |x_i| = |x_i|, \end{equation*}
where the first inequality holds because the absolute value of a sum is at most the sum of absolute values, and the last step uses that \(A\) is stochastic.
🔗
Therefore \(|\lambda x_i| \leq |x_i|\) and hence \(|\lambda| \leq 1\text{.}\) Finally, since \(A\) and \(A^T\) have the same eigenvalues, we have completed the proof.
