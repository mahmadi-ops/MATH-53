Theorem 6.34. Volume Equals the Absolute Determinant.

\begin{equation*} \operatorname{vol} P(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w}) = \left| \det \left[ \, \vec{u} \; | \; \vec{v} \; | \; \vec{w} \, \right] \right|. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Assume first that the base is honest, so \(\vec{n} = \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \neq \vec{0}\text{.}\) By Lemma 6.31, \(\vec{n}\) points along the perpendicular to the base plane. If \(\varphi\) is the angle between \(\vec{w}\) and \(\vec{n}\text{,}\) then the height of the box is the leg of a right triangle with hypotenuse \(\| \vec{w} \|\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} h = \| \vec{w} \| \, | \cos\varphi | = \| \vec{w} \| \, \frac{| \vec{w} \cdot \vec{n} |}{\| \vec{w} \| \| \vec{n} \|} = \frac{| \vec{w} \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) |}{\| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \|}. \end{equation*}
Therefore, by Lemma 6.30 and Lemma 6.32,
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{vol} = (\text{area of base}) \times h = \| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \| \cdot \frac{| \vec{w} \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) |}{\| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \|} = | \vec{w} \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) | = \left| \det \left[ \, \vec{u} \; | \; \vec{v} \; | \; \vec{w} \, \right] \right|. \end{equation*}
🔗
If instead \(\vec{u} \times \vec{v} = \vec{0}\text{,}\) then \(\sin\theta = 0\) by Lemma 6.30, so \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) are parallel (or one is \(\vec{0}\)), the box is flat, and \(\operatorname{vol} = 0\text{.}\) Consistently, the matrix has linearly dependent columns and its determinant is \(0\text{.}\) The theorem holds in all cases.
