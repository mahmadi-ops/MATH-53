Theorem 4.43. Matrix Inverses.

Let \(A\) be an \(n \times n\) matrix. Then the following conditions are equivalent.
🔗
  1. \(A\) is invertible.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The reduced row-echelon form of \(A\) is \(I\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\left[ \, A \mid I_n \, \right]\) can be transformed into \(\left[ \, I_n \mid A^{-1} \, \right]\) using the Matrix Inversion Algorithm.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Assume that \(\left[ \, A \mid I_n \, \right]\) can be transformed into \(\left[ \, I_n \mid B \, \right]\) using elementary row operations. This means the reduced row-echelon matrix of \(A\) is \(I\text{.}\)
🔗
Writing the columns of \(I_n\) as \(\vec{e}_1, \ldots, \vec{e}_n\) and the columns of \(B\) as \(\vec{B}_1, \ldots, \vec{B}_n\text{,}\) the reduction is
\begin{equation*} \left[ \, A \mid \vec{e}_1 \; \vec{e}_2 \; \cdots \; \vec{e}_n \, \right] \longrightarrow \left[ \, I_n \mid \vec{B}_1 \; \vec{B}_2 \; \cdots \; \vec{B}_n \, \right]. \end{equation*}
🔗
Then, for each \(i\text{,}\) the reduction \(\left[ \, A \mid \vec{e}_i \, \right] \longrightarrow \left[ \, I_n \mid \vec{B}_i \, \right]\) is possible, which means \(A \vec{x}_i = \vec{e}_i\) has the solution \(\vec{x}_i = \vec{B}_i\text{,}\) or \(A \vec{B}_i = \vec{e}_i\text{.}\) Hence
\begin{equation*} AB = \left[ A\vec{B}_1 \; A\vec{B}_2 \; \cdots \; A\vec{B}_n \right] = \left[ \vec{e}_1 \; \vec{e}_2 \; \cdots \; \vec{e}_n \right] = I. \end{equation*}
🔗
Later we will show that if \(AB = I\text{,}\) then \(BA = I\) and hence \(B = A^{-1}\text{.}\) This will be shown when we study Theorem 4.57.
