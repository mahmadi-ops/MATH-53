Lemma 6.30. Length of the Cross Product is an Area.

For all \(\vec{u}, \vec{v} \in \R^3\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \|^2 = \| \vec{u} \|^2 \| \vec{v} \|^2 - (\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v})^2, \end{equation*}
and consequently the parallelogram with edges \(\vec{u}\text{,}\) \(\vec{v}\) has area \(\| \vec{u} \| \| \vec{v} \| \sin\theta = \| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \|\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Expanding both sides in coordinates,
\begin{equation*} \| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \|^2 = \sum_{i \lt j} (u_iv_j - u_jv_i)^2, \qquad \| \vec{u} \|^2 \| \vec{v} \|^2 - (\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v})^2 = \Big( \sum_i u_i^2 \Big) \Big( \sum_j v_j^2 \Big) - \Big( \sum_i u_iv_i \Big)^2, \end{equation*}
and multiplying out the right-hand side, the terms \(u_i^2v_i^2\) cancel and the cross terms regroup as \(\sum_{i \lt j}(u_iv_j - u_jv_i)^2\) — an algebraic identity checked by direct expansion. Then, using \(\vec{u} \cdot \vec{v} = \| \vec{u} \| \| \vec{v} \| \cos\theta\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \|^2 = \| \vec{u} \|^2 \| \vec{v} \|^2 \left( 1 - \cos^2\theta \right) = \left( \| \vec{u} \| \| \vec{v} \| \sin\theta \right)^2, \end{equation*}
and both sides are nonnegative, so \(\| \vec{u} \times \vec{v} \| = \| \vec{u} \| \| \vec{v} \| \sin\theta\text{,}\) which is base times height for the parallelogram.
