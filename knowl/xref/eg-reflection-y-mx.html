Example 3.50. Reflection in the Line \(y = mx\).

The reflection in the line \(y = mx\) is a linear transformation. Find the matrix corresponding to this transformation.
🔗
Solution.
Let us denote the reflection in the line \(y = mx\) by \(Q_m : \R^2 \to \R^2\text{.}\) We need to compute \(Q_m(\vec{e}_1)\) and \(Q_m(\vec{e}_2)\text{.}\)
🔗
Let \(\theta\) be the angle the line \(y = mx\) makes with the positive \(x\)-axis. The point \((1, m)\) lies on the line and is at distance \(\sqrt{m^2+1}\) from the origin, so
\begin{equation*} \cos\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m^2+1}}, \qquad \sin\theta = \frac{m}{\sqrt{m^2+1}}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Reflecting \(\vec{e}_1\) in that line rotates it through \(2\theta\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} Q_m(\vec{e}_1) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(2\theta) \\ \sin(2\theta) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta \\ 2\sin\theta\cos\theta \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{m^2+1} \begin{bmatrix} 1 - m^2 \\ 2m \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Exercise. Show that \(Q_m(\vec{e}_2) = \dfrac{1}{m^2+1} \begin{bmatrix} 2m \\ m^2-1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and hence that the matrix corresponding to \(Q_m\) is
\begin{equation*} \frac{1}{m^2+1} \begin{bmatrix} 1 - m^2 \amp 2m \\ 2m \amp m^2 - 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
