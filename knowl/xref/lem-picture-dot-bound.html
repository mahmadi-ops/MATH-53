Lemma 7.58. The Dot Product Cannot Outrun the Lengths.

For all pictures \(X\) and \(Y\) in \(\mathbb{M}_{mn}\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} | X \cdot Y | \leq \| X \| \, \| Y \|, \end{equation*}
with equality exactly when one of the two pictures is a scalar multiple of the other.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
If \(Y\) is the zero picture both sides are \(0\) and \(Y = 0 \cdot X\text{,}\) so assume \(Y \neq 0\) and set \(Z = \| Y \|^2 X - (X \cdot Y) Y\text{.}\) Expanding \(\| Z \|^2 = Z \cdot Z\) by linearity in each argument,
\begin{equation*} \| Z \|^2 = \| Y \|^4 \| X \|^2 - 2 \| Y \|^2 (X \cdot Y)^2 + (X \cdot Y)^2 \| Y \|^2 = \| Y \|^2 \left( \| X \|^2 \| Y \|^2 - (X \cdot Y)^2 \right). \end{equation*}
The left side is a sum of squares, hence \(\geq 0\text{,}\) and \(\| Y \|^2 \gt 0\text{,}\) so \((X \cdot Y)^2 \leq \| X \|^2 \| Y \|^2\text{.}\) Taking square roots gives the inequality. Equality holds exactly when \(\| Z \|^2 = 0\text{,}\) that is, when \(Z\) is the zero picture, which says \(X = \frac{X \cdot Y}{\| Y \|^2} \, Y\text{.}\)
