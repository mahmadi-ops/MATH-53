The \(\sqrt{k}\) here is the additivity of variance, not the central limit theorem. At each pixel the \(k\) errors are independent with mean \(0\) and standard deviation \(\sigma\)—the grain model of Recognition by Nearest Reference, one error \(g_{ij}\) per pixel per shot—so their average \(\overline{g}_{ij}\) has standard deviation \(\sigma/\sqrt{k}\text{,}\) a two-line computation whose whole content is \(\operatorname{Var}(\overline{g}_{ij}) = \sigma^2/k\text{.}\) The \(k \to \infty\) version of the same fact is the law of large numbers. It says nothing about the shape of the error distribution; it is purely about its size. The central limit theorem, which concerns shape, appears in Assessing the Accuracy of the Verdicts*.
