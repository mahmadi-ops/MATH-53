Example 8.1. Can We Compute \(A^{1000}\) Efficiently?

Can we efficiently calculate \(A^{1000}\) if \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp -4 \end{bmatrix}\text{?}\)
🔗
Solution.
Consider the matrix \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 3 \end{bmatrix}\) and note that \(P^{-1} = \frac{1}{7} \begin{bmatrix} 3 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Let us compute \(P^{-1}AP\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} P^{-1}AP = \frac{1}{7} \begin{bmatrix} 3 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \\ 3 \amp -4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 3 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{7} \begin{bmatrix} 3 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 4 \amp 5 \\ 2 \amp -15 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp -5 \end{bmatrix} = D, \end{equation*}
a diagonal matrix.
🔗
How can the above calculation help us solve the original question? Rearranging,
\begin{align*} P^{-1}AP \amp = D\\ P \left( P^{-1}AP \right) P^{-1} \amp = PDP^{-1}\\ \left( PP^{-1} \right) A \left( PP^{-1} \right) \amp = PDP^{-1}\\ A \amp = PDP^{-1}. \end{align*}
🔗
And therefore the inner factors telescope:
\begin{align*} A^{1000} \amp = \left( PDP^{-1} \right)\left( PDP^{-1} \right) \cdots \left( PDP^{-1} \right)\\ \amp = PD \left( P^{-1}P \right) D \left( P^{-1}P \right) \cdots \left( P^{-1}P \right) DP^{-1}\\ \amp = P D^{1000} P^{-1}. \end{align*}
🔗
Powers of a diagonal matrix are trivial:
\begin{equation*} D^{1000} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp -5 \end{bmatrix}^{1000} = \begin{bmatrix} 2^{1000} \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp (-5)^{1000} \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
so
\begin{align*} A^{1000} \amp = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2^{1000} \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp (-5)^{1000} \end{bmatrix} \frac{1}{7} \begin{bmatrix} 3 \amp 1 \\ -1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{7} \begin{bmatrix} 3(2)^{1001} + 5^{1000} \amp 2^{1001} - 2(5)^{1000} \\ 3(2)^{1000} - 3(5)^{1000} \amp 2^{1000} + 6(5)^{1000} \end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
