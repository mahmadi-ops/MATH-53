Example 4.64 A Rotation as a Product of Three Shears.

Let \(-\pi \lt \theta \lt \pi\text{,}\) and let
\begin{equation*} A_{R_\theta} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \amp -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta \amp \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
be the matrix of the rotation \(R_\theta\) (Subsection 3.3.1).
🔗
  1. Row reduce \(A_{R_\theta}\) to \(I_2\) using only one kind of row operation: adding a multiple of one row to the other row. Use the result to write \(A_{R_\theta}\) as a product of shears.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Write out the factorization for a quarter turn, \(\theta = \pi/2\text{,}\) and check it by multiplying.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
(a) We follow the method of Example 4.63. If \(\theta = 0\text{,}\) then \(A_{R_\theta} = I_2\) and there is nothing to do. Otherwise \(\sin\theta \neq 0\text{.}\) The usual first step would scale the first row to make its leading entry \(1\text{.}\) Scaling is not allowed here, so we add \(t\) times the second row to the first row instead, and choose \(t\) so that the leading entry \(\cos\theta + t\sin\theta\) becomes \(1\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} t = \frac{1 - \cos\theta}{\sin\theta} = \tan\frac{\theta}{2}, \end{equation*}
by a half-angle identity. With this \(t\text{,}\) three row operations finish the reduction:
\begin{align*} \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \amp -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta \amp \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \amp \xrightarrow{\;tR_2 + R_1\;} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -t \\ \sin\theta \amp \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}\\ \amp \xrightarrow{\;-(\sin\theta) R_1 + R_2\;} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -t \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\\ \amp \xrightarrow{\;tR_2 + R_1\;} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
The two new entries need a short computation. In the first step, the second entry of the first row is
\begin{align*} -\sin\theta + t\cos\theta \amp = \frac{-\sin^2\theta + \cos\theta - \cos^2\theta}{\sin\theta}\\ \amp = \frac{\cos\theta - 1}{\sin\theta} = -t, \end{align*}
and in the second step, the last entry is \(\cos\theta + t\sin\theta = 1\text{,}\) by the choice of \(t\text{.}\)
🔗
The elementary matrices of the three steps are \(E_1 = H_t\text{,}\) \(E_2 = V_{-\sin\theta}\) and \(E_3 = H_t\text{,}\) and the reduction says that \(E_3 E_2 E_1 A_{R_\theta} = I_2\text{.}\) Therefore
\begin{equation*} A_{R_\theta} = E_1^{-1} E_2^{-1} E_3^{-1} = H_{-t}\, V_{\sin\theta}\, H_{-t}, \end{equation*}
where each inverse is found by inspection, as in Observation 4.62. Written out,
\begin{align*} \amp \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta \amp -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta \amp \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}\\ \amp \quad = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -\tan\frac{\theta}{2} \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ \sin\theta \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -\tan\frac{\theta}{2} \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
That is, \(A_{R_\theta} = H_a V_b H_a\) with \(a = -\tan\frac{\theta}{2}\) and \(b = \sin\theta\text{.}\) This also holds for \(\theta = 0\text{,}\) where \(a = b = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
(b) For \(\theta = \pi/2\) we get \(a = -\tan(\pi/4) = -1\) and \(b = \sin(\pi/2) = 1\text{,}\) so all the entries are integers:
\begin{align*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \amp = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\\ \amp = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}, \end{align*}
which is the matrix of the quarter turn in Example 3.47.
