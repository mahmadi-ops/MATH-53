Example 5.30 The Null Space of \(A\) and the Kernel of \(T_A\).

Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \\ 2 \amp 4 \\ -1 \amp -2 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and let \(T_A : \R^2 \to \R^3\) be its matrix transformation, \(T_A(\vec{x}) = A\vec{x}\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. Find \(\operatorname{Nul} A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Find \(\operatorname{Ker} T_A\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Find all vectors \(\vec{x} \in \R^2\) that \(T_A\) sends to the same output as \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
(a) We solve \(A\vec{x} = \vec{0}\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 2 \amp 4 \amp 0 \\ -1 \amp -2 \amp 0 \end{array} \right] \xrightarrow[r_1 + r_3]{-2r_1 + r_2} \left[ \begin{array}{cc|c} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{array} \right]. \end{equation*}
With \(y = t\) free, the first row gives \(x = -2t\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Nul} A = \operatorname{Span}\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}, \end{equation*}
a line through the origin in \(\R^2\text{.}\)
🔗
(b) By Definition 5.28, the kernel of \(T_A\) consists of the vectors \(\vec{x} \in \R^2\) with \(T_A(\vec{x}) = \vec{0}\text{,}\) that is, with \(A\vec{x} = \vec{0}\text{.}\) This is the equation solved in part (a), so
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Ker} T_A = \operatorname{Nul} A = \operatorname{Span}\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}. \end{equation*}
Parts (a) and (b) describe one set from two points of view: as the solutions of a homogeneous system whose coefficient matrix is \(A\text{,}\) and as the inputs that \(T_A\) sends to the zero vector. As a check,
\begin{equation*} T_A \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 + 2 \\ -4 + 4 \\ 2 - 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Notice that the kernel is a line in the domain \(\R^2\text{,}\) although the outputs of \(T_A\) are vectors in \(\R^3\text{.}\)
🔗
(c) First, \(T_A \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 6 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Since \(T_A\) is linear,
\begin{align*} T_A(\vec{x}) = T_A \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \amp \iff T_A \left( \vec{x} - \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \right) = \vec{0}\\ \amp \iff \vec{x} - \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \in \operatorname{Ker} T_A. \end{align*}
So the vectors we want are
\begin{equation*} \vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + t \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad t \in \R, \end{equation*}
the line through \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\) parallel to the kernel. For instance, \(t = 1\) gives \(\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and indeed
\begin{equation*} T_A \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 + 4 \\ -2 + 8 \\ 1 - 4 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 6 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
This is Theorem 2.24 in the language of transformations: two inputs have the same output exactly when they differ by a vector of the kernel. In particular \(T_A\) is not one-to-one, because its kernel contains nonzero vectors (Theorem 3.19).
