Example 3.50 Reflection in the \(x\)-Axis and in the Line \(y = x\).

Two reflections can be read directly from what they do to coordinates. In each case the columns of the matrix are \(T(\vec{e}_1)\) and \(T(\vec{e}_2)\text{.}\)
🔗
  • Reflection in the \(x\)-axis transforms \(\begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix}\) to \(\begin{bmatrix} a \\ -b \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Here \(T(\vec{e}_1) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(T(\vec{e}_2) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) so \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  • Reflection in the line \(y = x\) transforms \(\begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix}\) to \(\begin{bmatrix} b \\ a \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Here \(T(\vec{e}_1) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(T(\vec{e}_2) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) so \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Both are matrix transformations, \(T_A(\vec{x}) = A\vec{x}\) with \(\vec{x} \in \R^2\text{.}\)
