Example 3.4 A Matrix Transformation from \(\R^3\) to \(\R^4\).

Consider \(T : \R^3 \to \R^4\) defined by
\begin{equation*} T \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x + y \\ y \\ y - z \\ -y + z \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
For instance, \(T\) transforms \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \in \R^3\) to \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ -2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} \in \R^4\text{.}\) Separating the three variables shows that \(T\) is a matrix transformation:
\begin{equation*} T(\vec{x}) = x \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + y \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} + z \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp -1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{A} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}}_{\vec{x}} = T_A(\vec{x}). \end{equation*}
