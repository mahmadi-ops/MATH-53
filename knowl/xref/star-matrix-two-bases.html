Exercise 9. ★ The Same Matrix in Two Coordinate Systems.

In \(\mathbb{M}_{22}\text{,}\) let \(M = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp -1 \\ 3 \amp 5 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) let \(\mathcal{B}_1 = \{M_{11}, M_{12}, M_{21}, M_{22}\}\) be the standard ordered basis, and let
\begin{equation*} \mathcal{B}_2 = \left\{ \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \right\}. \end{equation*}
Find \([M]_{\mathcal{B}_1}\) and \([M]_{\mathcal{B}_2}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Relative to \(\mathcal{B}_1\text{.}\) The entries of \(M\text{,}\) read in order, are its coordinates:
\begin{equation*} M = 2M_{11} - M_{12} + 3M_{21} + 5M_{22}, \qquad [M]_{\mathcal{B}_1} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 3 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Relative to \(\mathcal{B}_2\text{.}\) Look for \(a, b, c, d\) with
\begin{equation*} M = a\begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} + b\begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} + c\begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} + d\begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Comparing entries one at a time: the \((1,2)\)-entry gives \(b = -1\text{;}\) the \((1,1)\)-entry gives \(a + b = 2\text{,}\) so \(a = 3\text{;}\) the \((2,1)\)-entry gives \(c = 3\text{;}\) and the \((2,2)\)-entry gives \(c + d = 5\text{,}\) so \(d = 2\text{.}\) Hence
\begin{equation*} [M]_{\mathcal{B}_2} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
The matrix \(M\) is one object; the two columns of numbers are two different descriptions of it.
