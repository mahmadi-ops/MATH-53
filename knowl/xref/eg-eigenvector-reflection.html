Example 8.7. A Geometric Eigenvector.

Consider reflection with respect to the \(x\)-axis in \(\R^2\text{,}\) with matrix \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and take \(\vec{x} = \vec{e}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = (1) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
so \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\) is an eigenvector of \(A\) with eigenvalue \(\lambda = 1\text{.}\) Geometrically this is obvious: points on the \(x\)-axis do not move under this reflection.
