Example 4.15. Computing a Product Column by Column.

Find the product \(AB\) of the matrices
\begin{equation*} A = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 0 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp -1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}_{2 \times 3} \qquad \text{and} \qquad B = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \\ 0 \amp -2 \amp 4 \\ 1 \amp 0 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}_{3 \times 3}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Solution.
Since \(A\) is \(2 \times 3\) and \(B\) is \(3 \times 3\text{,}\) the product \(AB\) is defined and has size \(2 \times 3\text{.}\) We compute \(AB_j\) for each of the three columns of \(B\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} AB_1 \amp = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 0 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp -1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix},\\ AB_2 \amp = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 0 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp -1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix},\\ AB_3 \amp = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 0 \amp 3 \\ 2 \amp -1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
🔗
Assembling these as the columns of the product gives
\begin{equation*} AB = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \amp -1 \amp -2 \\ -1 \amp 4 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}_{2 \times 3}. \end{equation*}
