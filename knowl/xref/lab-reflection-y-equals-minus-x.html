Exercise 22. Reflection in the Line \(y = -x\).

Let \(Q : \R^2 \to \R^2\) be the reflection in the line \(y = -x\text{.}\)
  1. Find the matrix of \(Q\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Compute \(Q^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
(a) Reflecting in \(y = -x\) swaps the two axes and reverses them: the point \((x,y)\) goes to \((-y,-x)\text{.}\) In particular \(\vec{e}_1 = (1,0) \mapsto (0,-1)\) and \(\vec{e}_2 = (0,1) \mapsto (-1,0)\text{,}\) so
\begin{equation*} Q = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ -1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
This is the case \(m = -1\) of Example 3.50.
🔗
(b) A reflection undoes itself, so \(Q^{-1} = Q\text{.}\) Indeed
\begin{equation*} Q^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ -1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ -1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{equation*} Q^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = Q \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
