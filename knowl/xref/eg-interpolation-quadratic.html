Example 1.37. A Parabola Through Three Points.

Find a polynomial \(p(x)\) of degree at most two whose graph passes through the points \((-1, 4)\text{,}\) \((1, 2)\) and \((3, 16)\text{,}\) and use it to estimate the value of \(p(2)\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
We look for
\begin{equation*} p(x) = r_0 + r_1 x + r_2 x^2. \end{equation*}
Substituting the three data points gives three linear equations in the unknowns \(r_0\text{,}\) \(r_1\text{,}\) \(r_2\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} p(-1) = r_0 - r_1 + r_2 \amp = 4\\ p(1) = r_0 + r_1 + r_2 \amp = 2\\ p(3) = r_0 + 3r_1 + 9r_2 \amp = 16. \end{align*}
🔗
We row reduce the augmented matrix:
\begin{align*} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 \amp -1 \amp 1 \amp 4 \\ 1 \amp 1 \amp 1 \amp 2 \\ 1 \amp 3 \amp 9 \amp 16 \end{array} \right] \amp \xrightarrow{\substack{-r_1 + r_2 \\ -r_1 + r_3}} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 \amp -1 \amp 1 \amp 4 \\ 0 \amp 2 \amp 0 \amp -2 \\ 0 \amp 4 \amp 8 \amp 12 \end{array} \right] \xrightarrow{\frac{1}{2} r_2} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 \amp -1 \amp 1 \amp 4 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 4 \amp 8 \amp 12 \end{array} \right]\\ \amp \xrightarrow{-4r_2 + r_3} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 \amp -1 \amp 1 \amp 4 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 8 \amp 16 \end{array} \right] \xrightarrow{\frac{1}{8} r_3} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 \amp -1 \amp 1 \amp 4 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{array} \right]\\ \amp \xrightarrow{\substack{r_2 + r_1 \\ -r_3 + r_1}} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 \amp 0 \amp 0 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 0 \amp -1 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{array} \right]. \end{align*}
🔗
Therefore \(r_0 = 1\text{,}\) \(r_1 = -1\text{,}\) \(r_2 = 2\text{,}\) and
\begin{equation*} p(x) = 1 - x + 2x^2. \end{equation*}
As a check, \(p(-1) = 1 + 1 + 2 = 4\text{,}\) \(p(1) = 1 - 1 + 2 = 2\text{,}\) and \(p(3) = 1 - 3 + 18 = 16\text{,}\) as required. Finally, the estimate at \(x = 2\) is
\begin{equation*} p(2) = 1 - 2 + 2(2^2) = 7. \end{equation*}
