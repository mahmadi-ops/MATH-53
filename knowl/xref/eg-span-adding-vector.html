Example 2.28. What Happens When We Add a Third Vector?

Consider the previous example, where the span of \(\vec{u}\) and \(\vec{v}\) was the \(yz\)-plane. Suppose we add another vector \(\vec{w}\text{,}\) and consider the span of \(\vec{u}\text{,}\) \(\vec{v}\text{,}\) and \(\vec{w}\text{.}\) What would happen to the span?
🔗
Solution.
Case (I): \(\vec{w}\) is also a vector in the \(yz\)-plane. For example, consider \(\vec{w} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Then \(\vec{w}\) is already in \(\operatorname{Span}\{ \vec{u}, \vec{v} \}\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \vec{w} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} = -5\vec{u} + 3\vec{v} = -5 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + 3 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Hence \(\operatorname{Span}\{ \vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w} \} = \operatorname{Span}\{ \vec{u}, \vec{v} \}\) — adding \(\vec{w}\) gained us nothing.
🔗
Case (II): \(\vec{w}\) is not in the \(yz\)-plane. For example, \(\vec{w} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Notice that any vector in \(\R^3\) can be written as a linear combination of \(\vec{u}\text{,}\) \(\vec{v}\text{,}\) and \(\vec{w}\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = x_1 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} + x_2 \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} + x_3 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*} x_1 = 2z - 3y - 3x, \qquad x_2 = 2x + 2y - z, \qquad x_3 = x. \end{equation*}
Hence \(\operatorname{Span}\{ \vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w} \} = \R^3\text{.}\)
🔗
The moral: a new vector enlarges the span exactly when it is not already in the span. This is the idea that the next section makes precise.
