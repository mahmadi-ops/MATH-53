Example 9.32. Pull One Cart and Let Go.

At time \(t = 0\text{,}\) the first cart is pulled one unit aside while the second is held at rest in place, and both are released from rest: in symbols, \(\vec{u}(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(\vec{u}\,'(0) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Find \(\vec{u}(t)\text{.}\) This is exactly the “messy” motion of Figure 9.28.
🔗
Solution.
Since both carts start at rest, every sine coefficient vanishes: the derivative of the general solution at \(t = 0\) is \(b_1 \omega_1 \vec{x}_1 + b_2 \omega_2 \vec{x}_2\text{,}\) and since \(\vec{x}_1, \vec{x}_2\) are linearly independent this is \(\vec{0}\) only when \(b_1 = b_2 = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
The cosine coefficients are the coordinates of the starting position in the eigenvector basis. We need
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = a_1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + a_2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
which gives \(a_1 = a_2 = \frac{1}{2}\text{.}\) Therefore
\begin{gather*} \vec{u}(t) = \frac{1}{2} \cos( \omega_1 t) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} + \frac{1}{2} \cos( \omega_2 t ) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \end{gather*}
or, cart by cart,
\begin{align*} u_1(t) \amp = \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(\omega_1 t) + \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(\omega_2 t), \\ u_2(t) \amp = \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(\omega_1 t) - \tfrac{1}{2}\cos(\omega_2 t). \end{align*}
🔗
The formula explains everything the movie shows. The second cart starts at rest, but its two cosines drift out of step and it begins to swing while the first cart calms down: the carts trade energy back and forth through the middle spring. And because \(\omega_2/\omega_1 = \sqrt{3}\) is irrational, the two cosines never return to step simultaneously—the motion never exactly repeats itself, even though it is built from two perfectly periodic ingredients. The animation runs precisely this solution: equal parts of the slow mode and the fast mode, with frequencies in the ratio \(\sqrt{3}\text{.}\)
