Lemma 6.32. The Scalar Triple Product is a Determinant.

For \(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w} \in \R^3\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \vec{w} \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = \det \begin{bmatrix} u_1 \amp v_1 \amp w_1 \\ u_2 \amp v_2 \amp w_2 \\ u_3 \amp v_3 \amp w_3 \end{bmatrix} = \det \left[ \, \vec{u} \; | \; \vec{v} \; | \; \vec{w} \, \right]. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Expanding the dot product,
\begin{equation*} \vec{w} \cdot (\vec{u} \times \vec{v}) = w_1(u_2v_3 - u_3v_2) + w_2(u_3v_1 - u_1v_3) + w_3(u_1v_2 - u_2v_1), \end{equation*}
and comparing with the cofactor expansion of \(\det [\, \vec{u} \mid \vec{v} \mid \vec{w} \,]\) along its third column (Definition 6.4): the three \(2 \times 2\) minors of the columns \(\vec{u}, \vec{v}\) are exactly the parenthesized factors, with matching signs. The two expressions agree term by term.
