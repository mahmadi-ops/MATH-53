Example 9.15. Why the Steady State Is Reached.

Prove the following statement: if \(T\) is a \(2 \times 2\) regular transition matrix then \(T\) has a steady state vector \(\vec{x}_*\) and furthermore for any initial vector \(\vec{x}_0\) we have \(\lim_{n \to \infty} T^n \vec{x}_0 = \vec{x}_*\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
A \(2 \times 2\) stochastic matrix is of the form
\begin{equation*} T = \begin{bmatrix} a \amp 1 - b \\ 1 - a \amp b \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \text{where } 0 \leq a \leq 1 \text{ and } 0 \leq b \leq 1. \end{equation*}
Note that if \(a = b = 0\text{,}\) then \(T = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(T^n\) alternates between \(T\) and \(I\text{,}\) hence \(T\) is not regular. Also, if \(a = b = 1\text{,}\) then \(T = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) which is clearly not regular. Therefore, we can ignore these two cases.
🔗
To compute \(T^n\text{,}\) we first diagonalize \(T\text{.}\) Computing the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors gives
\begin{equation*} \lambda_1 = 1, \quad \lambda_2 = a + b - 1, \qquad \vec{x}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} b - 1 \\ a - 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \vec{x}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*} T^n = PD^nP^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} b-1 \amp -1 \\ a-1 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp (a+b-1)^n \end{bmatrix} \frac{1}{a+b-2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 1 \\ 1-a \amp b-1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Now \(\lim_{n \to \infty} (a+b-1)^n = 0\text{,}\) since \(0 \lt a + b \lt 2\) and therefore \(-1 \lt a + b - 1 \lt 1\text{.}\) Therefore
\begin{equation*} \lim_{n \to \infty} T^n = \frac{1}{a+b-2} \begin{bmatrix} b-1 \amp b-1 \\ a-1 \amp a-1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Now, if the initial probability vector is \(\vec{x}_0 = \begin{bmatrix} p \\ 1-p \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) then
\begin{equation*} \lim_{n \to \infty} T^n \vec{x}_0 = \frac{1}{a+b-2} \begin{bmatrix} b-1 \amp b-1 \\ a-1 \amp a-1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} p \\ 1-p \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{a+b-2} \begin{bmatrix} b-1 \\ a-1 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
which is the probability vector corresponding to eigenvector \(\vec{x}_1\) with eigenvalue one, and is independent of \(p\text{.}\)
🔗
Summary.
  1. \(T_{2 \times 2}\) is a regular stochastic matrix.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\vec{x}_0\) is an initial probability vector.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. \(\vec{x}_*\) is a probability eigenvector of \(T\) corresponding to eigenvalue one.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. The steady state vector is \(\vec{x}_*\text{:}\) \(\lim_{n \to \infty} T^n \vec{x}_0 = \vec{x}_*\text{.}\) This means that to find the steady state vector of \(T_{2 \times 2}\text{,}\) we can find the eigenvector corresponding to eigenvalue one and normalize it.
