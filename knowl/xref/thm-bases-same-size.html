Theorem 5.16. Any Two Bases Have the Same Size.

Let \(H\) be a subspace of \(\R^n\) and suppose \(\{ \vec{u}_1, \ldots, \vec{u}_r \}\) and \(\{ \vec{v}_1, \ldots, \vec{v}_s \}\) are two bases for \(H\text{.}\) Then \(r = s\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Note that any spanning set has at least as many vectors as a basis, by Theorem 5.15. Also, any basis is itself a spanning set. Hence:
🔗
  1. \(\{ \vec{u}_1, \ldots, \vec{u}_r \}\) is a basis and \(\{ \vec{v}_1, \ldots, \vec{v}_s \}\) is a spanning set, therefore \(r \leq s\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. \(\{ \vec{v}_1, \ldots, \vec{v}_s \}\) is a basis and \(\{ \vec{u}_1, \ldots, \vec{u}_r \}\) is a spanning set, therefore \(s \leq r\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Together these give \(r = s\text{.}\)
