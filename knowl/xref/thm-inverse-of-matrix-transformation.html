Theorem 4.54 Composition and Inverses of Matrix Transformations.

  1. If \(A\) is an \(m \times n\) matrix and \(B\) is a \(p \times m\) matrix, then \(T_B \circ T_A = T_{BA}\text{:}\) composing matrix transformations corresponds to multiplying their matrices.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. If \(A\) is an invertible \(n \times n\) matrix, then \(T_A\) is invertible and \(\left( T_A \right)^{-1} = T_{A^{-1}}\text{:}\) the inverse matrix is the matrix of the inverse transformation.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.
(1) For every \(\vec{x} \in \R^n\text{,}\)
\begin{align*} (T_B \circ T_A)(\vec{x}) \amp = T_B\left( A\vec{x} \right) = B\left( A\vec{x} \right)\\ \amp = (BA)\vec{x} = T_{BA}(\vec{x}), \end{align*}
where the third equality is the associativity of matrix multiplication (Theorem 4.20), with \(\vec{x}\) regarded as an \(n \times 1\) matrix.
🔗
(2) By part (1), for every \(\vec{x} \in \R^n\text{,}\)
\begin{align*} \left( T_{A^{-1}} \circ T_A \right)(\vec{x}) \amp = \left( A^{-1}A \right)\vec{x} = I_n\vec{x} = \vec{x},\\ \left( T_A \circ T_{A^{-1}} \right)(\vec{x}) \amp = \left( AA^{-1} \right)\vec{x} = I_n\vec{x} = \vec{x}. \end{align*}
So \(T_{A^{-1}}\) is an inverse of \(T_A\text{.}\)
