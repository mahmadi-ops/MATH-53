Exercise 13. ★ Computing a Product Column by Column.

Find \(AB\text{,}\) where
\begin{equation*} A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp -1 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 3 \amp -2 \end{bmatrix}_{2 \times 3}, \end{equation*}
\begin{equation*} B = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp -1 \\ 2 \amp 1 \amp 3 \\ 0 \amp -2 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}_{3 \times 3}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Solution.
\(A\) is \(2 \times 3\) and \(B\) is \(3 \times 3\text{,}\) so \(AB\) is defined and is \(2 \times 3\text{.}\) Compute \(A\vec{B}_j\) for each column of \(B\text{:}\)
\begin{align*} A\vec{B}_1 \amp = A \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix},\\ A\vec{B}_2 \amp = A \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix},\\ A\vec{B}_3 \amp = A \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -5 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
🔗
Assembling these as columns,
\begin{equation*} AB = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \amp -5 \\ 7 \amp 7 \amp 6 \end{bmatrix}_{2 \times 3}. \end{equation*}
