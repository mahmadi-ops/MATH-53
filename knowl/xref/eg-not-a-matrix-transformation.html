Example 3.15 Transformations That Are Not Matrix Transformations.

Can you come up with an example of a transformation that is NOT a matrix transformation? Explain why it is not a matrix transformation.
🔗
Solution.
As an example, consider \(T : \R^2 \to \R^2\) defined by \(T(\vec{x}) = \vec{x} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) You can easily check that \(T\) does not preserve \(\vec{0}\text{,}\) since \(T(\vec{0}) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} \neq \vec{0}\text{,}\) and hence it is not a linear transformation.
🔗
Also, we cannot find a matrix \(A\) such that for all \(\vec{x} \in \R^2\) we have \(A\vec{x} = \vec{x} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Again consider \(\vec{x} = \vec{0}\text{.}\) Then \(\vec{0} = \vec{0} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) which is impossible.
🔗
As another example, consider \(T : \R^2 \to \R^2\) defined by \(T(\vec{x}) = \begin{bmatrix} x^2 \\ y \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) If such an \(A\) existed, then
\begin{equation*} A\vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} a \amp b \\ c \amp d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = x \begin{bmatrix} a \\ c \end{bmatrix} + y \begin{bmatrix} b \\ d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x^2 \\ y \end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
would require \(ax + by = x^2\) for all \(x, y\text{,}\) which is not possible.
