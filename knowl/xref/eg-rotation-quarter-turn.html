Example 3.47 A Quarter Turn.

The transformation \(R_{\pi/2} : \R^2 \to \R^2\) denotes a counterclockwise rotation about the origin through an angle of \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) radians. Find the matrix of \(R_{\pi/2}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
\begin{equation*} A_{R_{\pi/2}} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\frac{\pi}{2} \amp -\sin\frac{\pi}{2} \\ \sin\frac{\pi}{2} \amp \cos\frac{\pi}{2} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
so that
\begin{equation*} A_{R_{\pi/2}} \vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -y \\ x \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
For instance, with \(\vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\) we get \(R_{\pi/2}(\vec{x}) = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) As expected for a quarter turn, the dot product is zero:
\begin{equation*} \vec{x} \cdot R_{\pi/2}(\vec{x}) = (2)(-1) + (1)(2) = 0. \end{equation*}
