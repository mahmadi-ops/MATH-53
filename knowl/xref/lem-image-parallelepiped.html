Lemma 6.37. How \(T\) Transforms Parallelepipeds.

\(T\) maps \(P(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w})\) onto \(P(A\vec{u}, A\vec{v}, A\vec{w})\text{,}\) and
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{vol} T\big( P(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w}) \big) = | \det A | \cdot \operatorname{vol} P(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w}). \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
By linearity, \(T(t_1\vec{u} + t_2\vec{v} + t_3\vec{w}) = t_1A\vec{u} + t_2A\vec{v} + t_3A\vec{w}\) with the same coefficients \(t_i \in [0, 1]\text{:}\) the image of a parallelepiped under a linear map is again a parallelepiped, spanned by the images of its edges.
🔗
Now let \(V = \left[ \, \vec{u} \; | \; \vec{v} \; | \; \vec{w} \, \right]\text{.}\) The edge matrix of the image is \(\left[ \, A\vec{u} \; | \; A\vec{v} \; | \; A\vec{w} \, \right] = AV\text{.}\) By Theorem 6.34 and the product rule for determinants (Theorem 6.19),
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{vol} P(A\vec{u}, A\vec{v}, A\vec{w}) = | \det (AV) | = | \det A | \, | \det V | = | \det A | \cdot \operatorname{vol} P(\vec{u}, \vec{v}, \vec{w}). \end{equation*}
The entire geometric content is carried by one algebraic identity: \(\det(AV) = \det A \, \det V\text{.}\)
