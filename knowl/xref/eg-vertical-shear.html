Example 3.58 Vertical Shear.

A vertical shear \(T : \R^2 \to \R^2\) fixes \(\vec{e}_2\) and tilts \(\vec{e}_1\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} T(\vec{e}_2) = \vec{e}_2, \qquad T(\vec{e}_1) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ k \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
so that
\begin{equation*} A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ k \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
A shear leaves the vertical direction alone and slides each point vertically by an amount proportional to its \(x\)-coordinate, carrying a square to a parallelogram of the same area.
