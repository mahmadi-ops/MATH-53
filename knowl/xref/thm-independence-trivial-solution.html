Theorem 2.37. Independence and the Trivial Solution.

The collection of vectors \(\{ \vec{u}_1, \ldots, \vec{u}_k \}\) in \(\R^n\) is linearly independent if and only if whenever
\begin{equation*} \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_i \vec{u}_i = \vec{0} \end{equation*}
it follows that each \(a_i = 0\text{.}\)
🔗
Thus \(\{ \vec{u}_1, \ldots, \vec{u}_k \}\) in \(\R^n\) is linearly independent exactly when the system of linear equations \(AX = 0\) has only the trivial solution, where \(A\) is the \(n \times k\) matrix having these vectors as columns.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
If some \(a_i \neq 0\text{,}\) we may solve for \(\vec{u}_i\) in terms of the other vectors, exhibiting \(\vec{u}_i\) as a member of the span of the others. Conversely, if some \(\vec{u}_i\) lies in the span of the others, say \(\vec{u}_i = \sum_{j \neq i} c_j \vec{u}_j\text{,}\) then \(\sum_{j \neq i} c_j \vec{u}_j - \vec{u}_i = \vec{0}\) is a nontrivial relation, since the coefficient of \(\vec{u}_i\) is \(-1 \neq 0\text{.}\)
