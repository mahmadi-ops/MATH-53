Example 9.10. Every Stochastic Matrix Has Eigenvalue One.

Show that every stochastic matrix has eigenvalue one.
🔗
Solution.
(I) Note that \(\vec{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\) is an eigenvector of \(A^T\) with eigenvalue one. For example, with \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp \frac{1}{4} \\ 1 \amp \frac{3}{4} \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} A^T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp 1 \\ \frac{1}{4} \amp \frac{3}{4} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = (1) \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
Note that each entry of \(A^T \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ \vdots \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\) is equal to the sum of entries in a column of \(A\text{,}\) and since the columns of \(A\) are probability vectors, they add up to one.
🔗
(II) Also, \(\det(A - \lambda I) = \det \left( (A - \lambda I)^T \right) = \det(A^T - \lambda I)\text{,}\) which means \(A\) and \(A^T\) have the same eigenvalues.
🔗
From (I) and (II): \(A\) has eigenvalue one.
