Theorem 4.36. The Inverse of a \(2 \times 2\) Matrix.

Suppose \(A = \begin{bmatrix} a \amp b \\ c \amp d \end{bmatrix}\) and \(ad - bc \neq 0\text{.}\) Then
\begin{equation*} A^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad - bc} \begin{bmatrix} d \amp -b \\ -c \amp a \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Sketch.
Finding \(A^{-1}\) amounts to solving the two systems \(A \vec{x} = \vec{e}_1\) and \(A \vec{x} = \vec{e}_2\) at once, so we row reduce the augmented matrix
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{cc|cc} a \amp b \amp 1 \amp 0 \\ c \amp d \amp 0 \amp 1 \end{array} \right]. \end{equation*}
🔗
Replacing the first row by \(d R_1 - b R_2\) and the second by \(a R_2 - c R_1\) gives
\begin{equation*} \left[ \begin{array}{cc|cc} ad - bc \amp 0 \amp d \amp -b \\ 0 \amp ad - bc \amp -c \amp a \end{array} \right]. \end{equation*}
If \(ad - bc \neq 0\text{,}\) we may divide each row by \(ad - bc\) to obtain \(\left[ I_2 \mid A^{-1} \right]\) with \(A^{-1}\) as claimed. If instead \(ad - bc = 0\text{,}\) the reduction produces a row of zeros on the left, the systems are inconsistent, and no inverse exists.
