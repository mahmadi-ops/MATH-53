Theorem 4.33. Uniqueness of an Inverse.

If \(A\) is a square matrix and \(B\) and \(C\) are inverses of \(A\text{,}\) then \(B = C\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Since \(B\) and \(C\) are both inverses of \(A\text{,}\) we have
\begin{equation*} BA = AB = I \qquad \text{and} \qquad CA = AC = I. \end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{equation*} B = BI = B(AC) = (BA)C = IC = C, \end{equation*}
where the middle equality uses the associativity of matrix multiplication.
