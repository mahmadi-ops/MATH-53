Example 6.10. How Do Elementary Row Operations Affect Determinants?

Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \amp -3 \\ 0 \amp 4 \amp 0 \\ 1 \amp 0 \amp -2 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Computing \(\det A\) by cofactor expansion along row (or column) \(2\) yields
\begin{equation*} \det A = (4)(-1)^{2+2} \begin{vmatrix} 2 \amp -3 \\ 1 \amp -2 \end{vmatrix} = -4. \end{equation*}
🔗
Suppose \(B_1\text{,}\) \(B_2\text{,}\) and \(B_3\) are obtained from \(A\) by interchanging rows \(2\) and \(3\text{,}\) multiplying row \(3\) by \(-3\text{,}\) and adding twice row \(1\) to row \(3\text{,}\) respectively, i.e.,
\begin{equation*} B_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \amp -3 \\ 1 \amp 0 \amp -2 \\ 0 \amp 4 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \amp -3 \\ 0 \amp 4 \amp 0 \\ -3 \amp 0 \amp 6 \end{bmatrix}, \quad B_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp 0 \amp -3 \\ 0 \amp 4 \amp 0 \\ 5 \amp 0 \amp -8 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
How do elementary operations affect the determinant?
🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} \det B_1 \amp = (4)(-1)^{3+2} \begin{vmatrix} 2 \amp -3 \\ 1 \amp -2 \end{vmatrix} = -\det A,\\ \det B_2 \amp = (4)(-1)^{2+2} \begin{vmatrix} 2 \amp -3 \\ -3 \amp 6 \end{vmatrix} = -3 \det A,\\ \det B_3 \amp = (4)(-1)^{2+2} \begin{vmatrix} 2 \amp -3 \\ 5 \amp -8 \end{vmatrix} = (4)(-16 + 15) = \det A. \end{align*}
🔗
So interchanging rows flipped the sign, scaling a row scaled the determinant, and adding a multiple of one row to another left it alone.
