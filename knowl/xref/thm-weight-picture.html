Theorem 7.52. Every Linear Verdict Is a Weight Picture.

Let \(T \colon \mathbb{M}_{8,6} \to \R\) be any linear transformation. Then there is exactly one picture \(W\) with \(T(X) = X \cdot W\) for every \(X\text{,}\) namely the picture whose \((i,j)\) entry is \(T(E_{ij})\text{,}\) where \(\mathcal{E} = \{ E_{ij} \}\) is the pixel basis.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Every picture is a linear combination of the pixel basis, \(X = \sum_{i,j} x_{ij} E_{ij}\text{,}\) so linearity of \(T\) gives
\begin{equation*} T(X) = \sum_{i,j} x_{ij}\, T(E_{ij}). \end{equation*}
Let \(W\) be the picture with entries \(w_{ij} = T(E_{ij})\text{.}\) The sum on the right is then \(\sum_{i,j} x_{ij} w_{ij}\text{,}\) which is \(X \cdot W\) by Definition 7.47. For uniqueness, suppose \(T(X) = X \cdot V\) for every \(X\text{.}\) Feeding the machine a single lit pixel, \(X = E_{ij}\text{,}\) gives \(v_{ij} = E_{ij} \cdot V = T(E_{ij}) = w_{ij}\text{,}\) and this at every \((i,j)\) says \(V = W\text{.}\)
