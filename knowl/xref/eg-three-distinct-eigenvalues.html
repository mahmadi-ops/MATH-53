Example 8.14. Three Distinct Eigenvalues.

Find the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of \(B = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \amp -4 \amp 2 \\ 1 \amp -2 \amp 2 \\ 1 \amp -5 \amp 5 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Expanding \(\det(\lambda I - B)\) and factoring gives
\begin{equation*} \det(\lambda I - B) = (\lambda - 3)(\lambda^2 - 3\lambda + 2) = (\lambda - 3)(\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 2), \end{equation*}
so \(\lambda_1 = 1\text{,}\) \(\lambda_2 = 2\text{,}\) \(\lambda_3 = 3\text{.}\) They all have multiplicity one.
🔗
Finding the eigenvectors.
\begin{align*} \lambda_1 = 1: \amp \quad (I - B)\vec{x} = \vec{0} \; \longrightarrow \; \vec{x} = t \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix},\\ \lambda_2 = 2: \amp \quad (2I - B)\vec{x} = \vec{0} \; \longrightarrow \; \vec{x} = t \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix},\\ \lambda_3 = 3: \amp \quad (3I - B)\vec{x} = \vec{0} \; \longrightarrow \; \vec{x} = \tfrac{t}{2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}. \end{align*}
So \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\) are basic eigenvectors corresponding to \(\lambda_1 = 1\text{,}\) \(\lambda_2 = 2\text{,}\) and \(\lambda_3 = 3\text{.}\)
