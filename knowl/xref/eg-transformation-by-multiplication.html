Example 3.7 Transformation by Matrix Multiplication.

Consider the matrix \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 2 \amp 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) By matrix multiplication, \(A\) transforms vectors in \(\R^3\) into vectors in \(\R^2\text{.}\) Describe the effect on a general vector, and compute the image of \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Transforming a general vector by \(A\) looks like this:
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 2 \amp 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix}_{2 \times 3} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}_{3 \times 1} = \begin{bmatrix} x + 2y \\ 2x + y \end{bmatrix}_{2 \times 1}. \end{equation*}
For example,
\begin{equation*} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 2 \amp 1 \amp 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
