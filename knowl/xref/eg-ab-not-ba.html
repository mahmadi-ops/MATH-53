Example 4.19. Matrix Multiplication Is Not Commutative.

Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \\ 2 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(B = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 1 \\ 0 \amp 3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\)
  • Does \(AB\) exist? If so, compute it.
    🔗
    🔗
  • Does \(BA\) exist? If so, compute it.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
Both matrices are \(2 \times 2\text{,}\) so both products are defined and both are \(2 \times 2\text{:}\)
\begin{equation*} AB = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 1 \\ -2 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \text{whereas} \qquad BA = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp -1 \\ 6 \amp -3 \end{bmatrix}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Note that \(AB\) and \(BA\) are both defined; however, \(AB \neq BA\text{.}\) Matrix multiplication is not commutative.
