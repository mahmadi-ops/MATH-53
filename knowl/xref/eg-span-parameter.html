Example 2.33. When Does a Third Vector Fail to Enlarge the Span?

Let
\begin{equation*} \vec{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \vec{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \vec{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ h \\ -4 \end{bmatrix}, \end{equation*}
where \(h\) is a real number.
🔗
  1. For which values of \(h\) is \(\operatorname{Span}\{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \vec{v}_3\} = \R^3\text{?}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. For the exceptional value of \(h\text{,}\) describe the span by an equation and write \(\vec{v}_3\) explicitly in terms of \(\vec{v}_1\) and \(\vec{v}_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution.
(1) The three vectors span \(\R^3\) exactly when \(A\vec{x} = \vec{b}\) is consistent for every \(\vec{b}\text{,}\) where \(A = \begin{bmatrix} \vec{v}_1 \amp \vec{v}_2 \amp \vec{v}_3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) By Theorem 4.57, for a square matrix that happens exactly when \(A\) is invertible, i.e., when \(\det A \neq 0\text{.}\) Expanding along the first row,
\begin{align*} \det A \amp = \begin{vmatrix} 1 \amp 2 \amp 1 \\ 2 \amp 1 \amp h \\ -1 \amp 1 \amp -4 \end{vmatrix}\\ \amp = 1(-4 - h) - 2(-8 + h) + 1(2 + 1)\\ \amp = -4 - h + 16 - 2h + 3 = 15 - 3h. \end{align*}
So \(\det A = 0\) precisely when \(h = 5\text{,}\) and therefore
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Span}\{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \vec{v}_3\} = \R^3 \qquad \text{for every } h \neq 5. \end{equation*}
🔗
(2) Take \(h = 5\text{,}\) so \(\vec{v}_3 = (1,5,-4)\text{.}\) Solving \(a\vec{v}_1 + b\vec{v}_2 = \vec{v}_3\) gives \(a + 2b = 1\) and \(2a + b = 5\text{,}\) hence \(a = 3\) and \(b = -1\text{;}\) the third coordinate checks out, since \(-3 - 1 = -4\text{.}\) So
\begin{equation*} \vec{v}_3 = 3\vec{v}_1 - \vec{v}_2, \end{equation*}
which means \(\vec{v}_3\) adds nothing: \(\operatorname{Span}\{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \vec{v}_3\} = \operatorname{Span}\{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2\}\text{,}\) a plane.
🔗
A vector perpendicular to both \(\vec{v}_1\) and \(\vec{v}_2\) is \(\vec{n} = (1,-1,-1)\text{,}\) since \(\vec{n} \cdot \vec{v}_1 = 1 - 2 + 1 = 0\) and \(\vec{n} \cdot \vec{v}_2 = 2 - 1 - 1 = 0\text{.}\) Hence, when \(h = 5\text{,}\)
\begin{equation*} \operatorname{Span}\{\vec{v}_1, \vec{v}_2, \vec{v}_3\} = \left\{ (x,y,z) \in \R^3 \; : \; x - y - z = 0 \right\}, \end{equation*}
and indeed \(\vec{v}_3 = (1,5,-4)\) satisfies \(1 - 5 + 4 = 0\text{.}\)
