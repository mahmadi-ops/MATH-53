Diagonalization

Build a counterexample

The claim “Every real matrix with only positive eigenvalues is diagonalizable.”

Your goal

Construct a 3 × 3 matrix A that meets all three conditions:

  • All nine entries are nonzero integers.
  • det(tI − A) = (t − 1)2(t − 2)
    = t3 − 4t2 + 5t − 2.
  • dim ker(A − I) = 1.

The eigenvalue 1 repeats, but its eigenspace must have only one independent direction.

A place to start

Start with an upper triangular matrix J whose diagonal entries are 1, 1, and 2. Make its (1, 2) entry nonzero and all other off-diagonal entries zero. A change of basis, A = PJP−1, preserves its eigenvalues and eigenspace dimensions.

Try an integer matrix P with determinant 1 or −1, so its inverse also has integer entries. Choose the basis to make every entry of A nonzero.
