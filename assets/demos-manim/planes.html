Three planes and their common solutions

Predict: Each equation is a plane. Before rotating, decide what the three planes can have in common: nothing, one point, or a line. Then switch one plane off — what do the other two share?

The polygons show finite parts of the infinite planes, cut off by a viewing box. Every axis uses the same unit scale, and the view fits all plane corners as you rotate. Dashed gray lines are where two planes meet.

Camera controls

Drag the plot, focus it and use the arrow keys, or use these sliders. Reset restores the starting view and shows all planes.

How to read this figure
  1. Each equation a₁x₁ + a₂x₂ + a₃x₃ = d with not all aᵢ zero is a plane. A solution of the system is a point lying on every plane at once.
  2. Two planes that are not parallel meet in a line (dashed gray). A third plane either contains that line (infinitely many solutions), crosses it at one point (a unique solution), or is parallel to it and misses it (no solution).
  3. When the common solutions form a line, the slider moves a point along it; the readout substitutes the point into every visible equation and checks that each is satisfied.
  4. Hiding a plane removes its equation from the system, so the readout always describes the solution set of the equations that are visible.
